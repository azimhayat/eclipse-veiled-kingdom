# Eclipse of the Veiled Kingdom — Core Cast Visual Bible

Status: concept review pass. Nothing in this folder is integrated into the game, locked as final production art, or published.

## Shared visual language

- Premium dark-fantasy stylised realism with painterly materials, matching Aren's established silhouette and Orun's monumental architecture.
- Human characters remain recognisably human. Moral alignment is communicated through posture, materials, light, and symbols rather than monster anatomy.
- Dark navy and charcoal identify the eclipsed kingdom. Warm gold identifies memory, oath, restoration, and interactable truth. Cyan identifies living memory and Liora's distributed presence. Restrained vermilion identifies imposed authority and the inverted Crown Engine.
- Costumes are functional, layered, weathered, and readable at gameplay scale. No sexualised armour, ornamental high heels, oversized fantasy weapons, or generic royal pageantry.
- A silhouette must remain recognisable when reduced to the same on-screen height as Aren. Fine surface detail supports close-ups but cannot carry identity alone.
- The split-circle Cartographer seal, hollow-crown ring, Last Lamp, divided oath-cloak, memory fractures, and civic scales form the principal recurring motifs.
- Concept sheets use a neutral dark studio ground without labels. Typography and UI are added separately so generated lettering cannot become false canon.

## Scale and production rules

- Aren defines the base human scale: 1.00.
- Liora: 0.96; Mira: 0.94; Serath: 1.03; Ysra: 1.00.
- Warden of Dust: approximately 2.8 times Aren's height in narrative views, compressed only when gameplay readability requires it.
- Nameless Magistrate: approximately 1.8 times Aren's height.
- Every production character will ultimately need front, three-quarter, side, back, neutral, locomotion, interaction, combat, damage, and restoration references.
- Concept approval comes before sprite extraction, skeletal animation, cinematic staging, or replacement of existing art.

## Core cast

### Aren Vale — the Veilbound

A vulnerable human Crown Cartographer, not a superhero. Sand-gold hood and torn mantle, dark layered field clothes, practical bracers, a cyan memory compass at the belt, and a broken oath-blade. His face is initially hidden by the hood, but his body language must show fatigue, accountability, and persistence.

### Princess Liora of Orun — the Erased Heir

An adult reformer and co-protagonist, never a captive or reward. Her field-practical civic coat combines deep navy, restrained gold seams, and cyan living-light fractures. She wears no crown. A concealed split-circle seal identifies her link to Aren's maps. Human and living-light presentations must preserve the same face, proportions, posture, and clothing logic. Early appearances may obscure her identity, but not erase her agency.

### Mira of the Last Lamp — guide and dissenter

An older living archivist with a compassionate but burdened face. Layered indigo and ash robes are soot-stained from maintaining sanctum flames. Her brass-and-stone lamp staff is a working archival instrument, not a wizard prop. Warm amber light distinguishes chosen testimony from the Crown's imposed light.

### Regent Serath — the Hollow Crown

A middle-aged human regent whose threat comes from controlled certainty. Tailored black-navy ceremonial armour and robes form a narrow, disciplined silhouette. A hollow architectural ring sits behind rather than upon his head. Thin vermilion command lines suggest the inverted Engine. He must look capable of sincere mercy and deliberate coercion at the same time—never like an undead warlord.

### Captain Ysra — the divided oath

An adult veteran commander and Liora's former sworn protector. Her practical gunmetal/navy armour supports real movement. A divided cloak carries vermilion occupation colour outside and faded Liora-blue within. Her equipment combines a reach weapon with a short defensive blade. Protection, pursuit, doubt, and earned change must all be readable in her posture.

## Chapter guardians

### Warden of Dust — Chapter I guardian

A monumental civic guardian built from stone, bronze, compacted dust, and vow-inscribed mechanisms. In the commanded state a vermilion bridle constrains its head and cyan-gold heart. In restoration the bridle breaks, the heart opens, and the same body becomes protective rather than docile. It is not killed; Aren breaks the Crown's command.

### The Nameless Magistrate — Chapter II guardian

A civic adjudicator whose own identity has been erased. A tall articulated stone-and-cloth body carries paired scales, route chains, a blank face-seal, gold legal geometry, and cyan fractures where a name should be. Its silhouette belongs to a court and a gate, not a final throne. Restoration should reveal personhood without simply turning it into an ordinary human.

## Occupation enemy family

Ordinary opponents are citizens trapped inside Serath's command structure, not disposable monsters. Their shared uniform begins with the same dark navy, oxidised bronze and weathered cloth seen across Orun, then adds a restrained vermilion command bridle at the collar and sternum. Cyan memory leakage appears only during recoveries, guard breaks, or liberation moments.

### Crown infantry

One modular family supports the established combat roles without making every soldier identical:

- **Striker:** light armour, short command sabre, fast readable three-beat pressure.
- **Spear guard:** medium armour, long reach, triangular shoulder profile and clear forward wind-up.
- **Shield keeper:** heavy front silhouette, civic door-shield and protected command seal that must be guard-broken.
- **Memory archer:** narrow ranged silhouette, compact Orun bow and cyan-charged route arrows; never a modern firearm.

Faces remain partly visible and individually human. Helmets are practical open-faced civic equipment, not anonymous skull masks. Silhouette, weapon and shoulder profile must communicate combat role before colour or fine detail.

### Veil paratroopers

Paratroopers belong to the same occupation family but wear a compact folded veil-wing descent rig derived from Orun's ceremonial canopy architecture. The rig opens into two weathered cloth-and-bronze surfaces above and behind the body, then folds tightly after landing so melee movement remains readable. Amber descent tells and cyan recovery windows belong to gameplay effects, not permanent costume glow.

The descent apparatus must never resemble a modern parachute, military aircraft harness, angel wing, bat wing, or science-fiction jetpack. A paratrooper remains recognisable as the same human soldier before, during and after descent.

## Deliberately deferred

- The Choir Below is a collective visual system, not one representative character.
- Talen, Rook, Sef, named citizens, occupation families, and later guardians require a chapter-by-chapter cast map before final design.
- Romance-specific imagery for Aren and Liora is deferred until their individual identities and shared political history are visually approved.

## Review gate

Approve or revise five things for each character: silhouette, apparent age, emotional tone, colour ownership, and story symbolism. Only then should the chosen design become a reusable production model for sprites and cinematics.

## Image-generation prompt record

All concepts in this review pass are original, generated locally through the built-in image-generation workflow. The established Aren sheet is used only as the internal style and proportion anchor; the title still is used only as the Orun palette and material reference. No external characters, franchises, or downloaded art are used.
