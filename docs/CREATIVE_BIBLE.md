# Eclipse of the Veiled Kingdom — Creative Bible

## North star

Build a cinematic action-adventure platformer about recovering a kingdom's stolen memory. The player should feel agile, vulnerable, curious, and increasingly capable. Every room must deliver at least one of four values: mastery, discovery, spectacle, or story.

The initial release contains 100 authored levels arranged as ten realms of ten chapters. Additional realms can be added later without changing the central rules or invalidating saved progress.

## Player promise

> Enter a kingdom erased by an artificial eclipse, recover the memories hidden inside its ruins, and decide whether its lost light deserves to return.

## Creative pillars

1. **Movement with intention** — immediate controls, readable momentum, and traversal challenges that reward mastery rather than trial-and-error.
2. **The world remembers** — architecture, enemies, relics, light, and sound reveal history without relying on long exposition.
3. **Light changes truth** — illumination is navigation, story, combat language, and the visual signature of the game.
4. **Every realm transforms the rules** — each ten-level arc introduces one mechanic, develops it, combines it with earlier skills, and ends with a bespoke guardian.
5. **No filler** — repeated geometry, palette swaps, or stat-only difficulty increases do not qualify as new levels.

## Story premise

The Kingdom of Orun built the Crown Engine to preserve every citizen's memories in living light. During a succession war, Regent Serath inverted the machine. The resulting eclipse buried the capital, erased names from history, and turned remembered duties into hostile shades.

The hero, **Aren Vale**, awakens beneath the Outer Veil carrying a broken relic sword and a single impossible memory: the sun rising over a kingdom that official history says never existed. Aren is a former Crown Cartographer—one of the people who helped map memories into the Engine—but does not initially remember that responsibility.

The player gathers **Dawn Fragments**, restores the ten Crown Paths, and discovers that the eclipse is not merely Serath's weapon. It is also holding back a catastrophic archive of unresolved grief. Restoring the sun without healing the archive would destroy what remains of Orun.

## Main cast

- **Aren Vale — the Veilbound:** precise, resourceful, and haunted by missing choices. His cloak records recovered constellations as the campaign progresses.
- **Mira of the Last Lamp — guide and dissenter:** a living archivist who communicates through sanctum flames. She wants Orun restored but conceals the cost of the original Crown Engine.
- **Regent Serath — the Hollow Crown:** the visible antagonist. He believes forgetting pain is mercy and uses the eclipse to keep the kingdom stable and obedient.
- **The Choir Below — collective memory:** voices stored in the Engine. Sometimes helpful, sometimes monstrous, never entirely trustworthy.
- **Captain Ysra — commander of the occupation:** Aren's former ally. She hunts him across the middle realms and becomes a recurring rival rather than a simple villain.

## Narrative structure

- **Act I — Remember the path (Levels 1–20):** Aren learns the kingdom existed and that he once served it.
- **Act II — Remember the crime (Levels 21–50):** the Crown Engine's purpose and Serath's coup are revealed.
- **Act III — Remember the self (Levels 51–70):** Aren confronts the harm caused by his own maps and loses Mira's guidance.
- **Act IV — Choose what survives (Levels 71–90):** the dead, the occupation, and the rebellion offer incompatible futures.
- **Act V — Break the perfect memory (Levels 91–100):** Aren reaches the throne and chooses restoration, release, or a difficult synthesis.

## Visual language

### Style

Cinematic stylized realism with painterly materials and physically believable light. Characters and gameplay objects must retain strong silhouettes at play scale. Backgrounds may be richly detailed, but the traversable plane remains readable through value separation and controlled color.

### Composition

- Dark navy and charcoal establish negative space.
- Warm gold marks the critical path, interactable mechanisms, and restored memory.
- Cyan indicates forgotten or unstable memory.
- Vermilion signals immediate danger and hostile authority.
- The active gameplay plane must be at least one value step brighter than the background behind it.
- Foreground occlusion may frame a scene but must never conceal hazards or objectives.

### Hero consistency

- Aren is human-scale: roughly two world tiles tall visually, aligned with his collision body.
- The sand-gold hood and torn cloak are the primary silhouette.
- The sword produces a short crescent of recovered light; the tool hand produces granular cyan sparks.
- Every animation preserves body scale, foot contact, facing direction, and weapon placement.

### Environment standards

- Each realm receives a palette card, material sheet, architectural motif, atmospheric effect, and unique horizon landmark.
- Generated images are source material, not automatic final assets. They must be inspected, cropped, color-matched, performance-optimized, and tested behind gameplay.
- No text, logos, watermarks, photographic UI, or modern real-world objects appear inside the world art.

### Interface

The UI should feel like an illuminated field instrument: thin gold rules, dark translucent glass, restrained serif headings, and highly legible sans-serif gameplay text. Information appears only when actionable. Touch controls must preserve the world view and support landscape play.

## Audio language

- A low vocal and bowed-metal motif represents stored memory.
- Frame drums and processed impacts represent the occupation.
- Restored areas add human breath, strings, and brighter harmonic intervals.
- Each mechanic has a distinct pre-action cue, action sound, and success confirmation.
- Music should respond to exploration, danger, revelation, guardian phases, and sanctuary rather than loop unchanged.

## Level quality contract

Every shippable level must have:

- A one-sentence dramatic purpose and a one-sentence mechanical purpose.
- A readable opening composition and a visible short-term objective.
- Introduction, development, twist, and payoff for its primary mechanic.
- At least one authored discovery, vista, or character beat.
- A critical path completable without damage by a skilled player.
- Checkpoint placement validated against actual completion time and difficulty.
- Keyboard, controller, and touch completion evidence.
- No softlocks, blind jumps, unavoidable damage, unclear interaction language, or irreversible puzzle failure.
- Stable performance at the agreed desktop and mobile budgets.

## Definition of world-class for this project

World-class is not the number of levels or the amount of visual detail. It means the controls remain trustworthy, the art belongs to one world, the story earns attention, the challenge teaches before it tests, and the player consistently understands why they want to take the next step.
